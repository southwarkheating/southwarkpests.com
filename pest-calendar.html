The pest calendar: when each pest is most active

Pests follow the seasons. Knowing what is coming lets you act early, when problems are smaller and easier to solve.

Pests by season

SeasonWhat to watch forWhat to do
Spring (March to May)Wasp queens starting nests, ants emerging, fleas hatching as it warms, squirrels breeding in loftsSeal roof gaps, check lofts and sheds, treat pets for fleas
Summer (June to August)Wasps, flying ants, flies, bed bugs after holidays, mothsCover food outdoors, clean bins, wash luggage after travel
Autumn (September to November)Rodents moving indoors, cluster flies, spiders, late-season waspsSeal entry points, remove food sources, check lofts
Winter (December to February)Rats and mice indoors, fox breeding season, indoor pests in warm buildingsCheck for gnawing and droppings, secure bins

Year-round indoor pests

Cockroaches, bed bugs, pharaoh ants, clothes moths and carpet beetles can be active all year in heated homes. Seasonal patterns matter less for these.

Why early action helps

Pest populations grow quickly. One pair of mice can become dozens within months, and a wasp nest grows from a golf ball to a football. Acting when you first see signs is cheaper, simpler and less stressful than waiting.

Month by month

MonthWhat we see most in South London
January to FebruaryMice and rats indoors, squirrels in lofts, foxes noisy during the breeding season
March to AprilWasp queens waking, ants appearing, squirrels breeding, gulls returning to roofs
May to JuneBee swarms, early wasp nests, flying insects, pigeons nesting
July to AugustWasp nests at their largest, flying ant days, fleas, flies, bed bugs after holidays
September to OctoberLate wasps, cluster flies, house spiders, rodents starting to move indoors
November to DecemberRats and mice in lofts, walls and kitchens, clothes moths in heated homes
