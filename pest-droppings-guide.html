Pest droppings: how to tell what you have

Droppings are often the first and clearest sign of pests. Their size, shape and where you find them usually tell you exactly what you are dealing with.

How to use this guide

Look at where the droppings are, how many there are, and how fresh they look. Fresh droppings are dark and soft. Old droppings are grey, dry and crumbly. Wear gloves, avoid dry sweeping and wash your hands afterwards.

Bats: a protected species

If droppings crumble to a powder of tiny shiny fragments and appear in clusters under a roof edge, you may have bats. All bats and their roosts are legally protected in the UK. Do not seal, disturb or treat a suspected roost. Contact the Bat Conservation Trust helpline for advice before any work.

What to do next

Once you have a rough idea, check for entry points, remove food sources and use traps or professional treatment as appropriate. If you are unsure, take a photo with a coin or ruler for scale and send it to us on the contact page.

Where you find droppings matters

  • Kitchen drawers, under the sink, behind the cooker: usually mice
  • Loft insulation, along joists: mice, rats or squirrels, depending on size
  • Along the edges of a garden path or by a shed: rats
  • Mattress seams and headboards: bed bugs
  • Hinges of kitchen cupboards and behind fridges: cockroaches

Fresh or old?

Fresh rodent droppings are dark, soft and shiny. After a few days they dry, turn dull and crumble when pressed with a stick. If you clear all the droppings and new ones appear, the pests are still active. That is a useful check before and after treatment.
