Pest guides · Wasps and bees

Wasps or bees? How to tell the difference

Wasps can be treated, but bees are vital pollinators and should be left alone or moved by a beekeeper. Here is how to tell them apart.

Behaviour

Wasps scavenge for sugar and meat, so they come to picnics, bins and drinks, especially in late summer. Bees visit flowers and are rarely interested in your food. Bumblebees are gentle and very unlikely to sting unless handled.

Why we do not treat bees

Honeybees and bumblebees are essential pollinators and their numbers are under pressure. We only consider treatment where bees are in a dangerous position and cannot be safely removed by a beekeeper. For honeybee swarms, see what to do about a bee swarm.

Questions and answers

Do bees sting like wasps?

Honeybees can sting once and then die. Bumblebees rarely sting. Wasps can sting repeatedly.

Do bumblebee nests last all year?

No. A bumblebee colony lasts one season and dies off by late summer or autumn.

What are the small holes in my brickwork?

Usually solitary masonry bees, which are harmless. Repointing soft mortar in winter stops them using it.
